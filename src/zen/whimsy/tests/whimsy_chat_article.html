Why cities are dropping parking rules

Northline · By Dana Ruiz · 9 min read

Across the country, more than 60 cities have now removed the rule that every new building must come with a set number of parking spaces.

Buffalo was first in 2017. Builders there added 21% fewer spaces on average, and most of the land went to homes and shops instead.

Critics worry about crowded streets. In Minneapolis, a city study found street parking stayed under 85% full on most blocks after the change.

The biggest effect is on cost: a single garage space can add $50,000 to the price of a new apartment, and renters pay for it whether they own a car or not.

Planners say the change works best alongside better buses and protected bike lanes, so that fewer people need a car for every trip.
